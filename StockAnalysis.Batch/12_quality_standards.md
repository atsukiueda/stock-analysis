# Quality Standards

> **APPROVED AND RELEASED.** This Standard is effective for its stated scope.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This Standard defines common quality direction for project documentation, knowledge, development, models, decision systems, and operations. It establishes quality attributes and routing expectations; domain-specific criteria, metrics, and methods remain delegated.

## 2. Quality principles

Quality is evidence-based, traceable, explainable where applicable, proportionate to governed risk and change, independently reviewable, and maintainable over time. No single metric, test result, or approval substitutes for the applicable quality controls.

## 3. Quality attributes

| Attribute | Direction |
|---|---|
| Correctness | Fit to applicable governed purpose and evidence |
| Traceability | Links among authority, evidence, decision, work product, and outcome |
| Explainability | Understandable behavior and rationale where applicable |
| Maintainability | Long-term clarity, controlled change, and manageable dependencies |
| Reliability | Appropriate predictable behavior and controlled failure handling |
| Reproducibility | Repeatable evaluation or operation at the required level |
| Security and integrity | Protection of governed boundaries and material records through applicable controls |
| Usability | Understandable, actionable outputs for their intended participants |

## 4. Domain routing

Documentation quality follows Documentation Governance and Workflow. Knowledge and research quality follow existing Knowledge Base and research authorities. Development quality follows Development Governance, Coding Standards, Workflow, and Testing & Validation. Model and decision-system quality follow Model Governance and its Control Family. Investment validation remains distinct and is routed to applicable future Investment Governance.

## 5. Evidence and delegation

Quality claims preserve applicable scope, evidence, validation context, reviewer context, and disposition relationships. Metrics, thresholds, scorecards, test methods, monitoring methods, security controls, and remediation procedures remain delegated to applicable lower-level controls.
